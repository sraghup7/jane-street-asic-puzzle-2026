# Net 806 — the chip's one undriven net, characterised

*Investigation 2026-09-13, after the Step-5 freeze. Trigger: on the `TWO NOT TOUCH` path, one character
of the printed message depends on this net, and neither tie reproduces the published string. The
question was whether that is a property of the design or a defect in our extraction.*

## What is established

**1. It is the chip's only undriven net, and the extraction asserts it rather than assuming it away.**
`recon/derived/netlist_check.json` totals: `undriven_nets: 1`,
`undriven_nets_with_multiple_terminals: 1`, `non_port_nets_without_a_driver: 1`, and B5's own method
note explains why it is asserted *by enumeration* (a named net with its terminals) rather than by a
"no net is undriven" claim, "which could only ever have been met by inventing a driver".

Its terminals are exactly two pins, both inputs, both on li1 (67/20):

| instance | master | pin | coordinate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `i0523` | `sky130_fd_sc_hd__a31oi_2` | `A1` | (175005, 90950) |
| `i0525` | `sky130_fd_sc_hd__a311o_2` | `A1` | (179165, 91290) |

No pin on the net has an output role, so nothing in the layout drives it.

**2. Our own artifact records that this net once had a *fabricated* driver — and that B6 caught it.**
`netlist_check.json` → `method.why_not_structure_only`, verbatim:

> the structural rules enforce "a net needs a driver" by mechanisms that can manufacture one. That is
> how an impossible direction (`a31oi_2::A1` as an output, when the family name says Y) got into this
> artifact, and the fabrication then certified its own check. **B6 caught it.**

The cross-check block still carries the disagreement:

```
decided_by_structure_alone        : 284
undetermined_by_structure_alone   : [a31oi_2::Y, o32ai_2::A2]
structure_contradicts_the_labels  : [a31oi_2::A1]      <- the pin on this net
```

So the structural evidence says *something must drive net 806*, and the cheapest way to satisfy it was
to call one of the two readers' `A1` an output. The pin labels say otherwise (the family's output is
`Y`), and we kept the labels, recorded the contradiction, and let the net be undriven.

**3. The constant-cell hypothesis is refuted.** The design does use tie cells: six
`sky130_fd_sc_hd__conb_1` instances, each with a `HI` and a `LO` output — 6 of those 12 outputs drive
real nets and 6 are legitimately dangling (`unused_output`, listed in
`netlist_check.json.single_terminal_nets`). But the nearest conb output to net 806 is **9 966 dbu
(~10 µm) away**. Net 806 is not tied to a constant through a constant cell.

**4. Its geometry is coherent with our pin model.** `a31oi_2`'s `A1` carries a master-local wire on
67/20 (rect 1.955,0.995 → 2.665,1.615 µm) plus two 67/16 pin rects, so a met1 wire landing on the pin
is what the model expects. Nothing about the two pin rectangles is anomalous.

**5. The pin *rule* is warm-up-validated; the per-master assignments are not.** All **69** puzzle
masters are absent from the warm-up netlist by name, so A4's calibration validated the *rule* on the
warm-up's masters and applied it to 69 different ones. A5 records the coverage honestly: 560 pins
named, modelled and with geometry; 489 reach a routing layer; 71 exceptions with stated causes (well
ties with no routeable geometry, antenna-diode supply squares); 87.69 % of placed pins have routing
geometry.

## What this means for the message

With the net genuinely undriven, our model reports `?` for the bits that depend on it, and forcing the
net decides them:

| 806 | message our model prints |
|---|---|
| 0 | `TWO"NOT TOUCH` (character 3: space → `"`) |
| 1 | `TWO NOT TOUCJ..` (character 12: `H` → `J`) |

**Neither equals the published `TWO NOT TOUCH`.** Because one bit needs 806 = 1 and the other needs
806 = 0, no value of a single net can produce the published string — so **at least one of those two
dependencies is a defect in our model, not a property of the design.** That converts an open question
into a specific, testable claim:

> one of the two message bits we route through `806` is mis-modelled — most plausibly through the one
> unvalidated link in the chain, the per-master `A1`/`Y` pin assignment for `a311o_2` / `a31oi_2`,
> which the warm-up cannot check because it contains neither master.

## The decisive experiment — run 2026-09-13

**Method.** Rebuild the extraction engine from A1/A2 alone (the same `connect.build_engine` call the
pipeline makes), probe the geometry at the two recorded pin coordinates to recover the net, file
**every shape on the die** by the net the engine assigns it, then intersect that net's own region with
every *cut* layer — a via landing on the wire but belonging to another net is the one defect that could
hide a driver — and with every other conductor layer. Script `recon/scratch/n806_overlay.py`, output
`recon/scratch/n806_overlay_out.txt`.

**Result.**

- Both pins probe to cluster **806** — the engine independently reproduces the numbering in
  `pin_net.json`.
- Net 806 is **17 shapes on five `(layer, datatype)` pairs** — li1 (67/20 ×4, 67/44 ×2), met1
  (68/20 ×6, 68/44 ×2) and met2 (69/20 ×3) — bbox `(174895,90450)-(179330,91875)` ≈ 4.4 µm × 1.4 µm: a
  *routed* net across three metal levels, not a stub, with **four of its own vias** (67/44 ×2 = li1↔met1,
  68/44 ×2 = met1↔met2), every one `SAME NET`.
- **The missed-merge hypothesis is refuted.** Exactly one cut overlaps the net and belongs elsewhere:
  `68/44 (174955,90875;175105,91025) → net 766`, which is `i0523`'s own output `Y`. It is a met1↔met2
  via landing inside the net's **li1** rectangle — different layers, no shared cut — so it is a
  legitimate underlap, not a merge opportunity.
- Conductors overlapping the net's footprint on other layers belong to nets 766/784/789/808 —
  crossings, again legal.

**Verdict: net 806 is genuinely undriven in the layout.** Three hypotheses have now been tested and
killed — a constant cell ties it (nearest `conb` ≈ 10 µm away); our pin geometry mis-assigns its
terminals (the engine reproduces the same two terminals, and the net's own vias sit on those pins'
local-wire shapes); a missed via merge hides its driver (the only overlapping cut is on other layers).
The pin assignment is now **corroborated by the router's own via placement**, which is stronger than
what this note was able to say when it opened.

**The constant-tie logic flaw.** The two forced readings do not settle the question by themselves.
Forcing net 806 to 0 gives `TWO"NOT TOUCH` (character 3 off), forcing it to 1 gives `TWO NOT TOUCJ..`
(character 12 off), and the earlier framing of that — "no single *constant* value prints the published
string, so the class stays unmistakable but byte-exactness is unattainable" — was correct as far as it
went and incomplete as a conclusion. A constant is not the only thing a lost wire could have carried.
The question that actually settles it is whether an *existing signal*, routed to these two consumers by
a connection our extraction missed, reproduces the message — not whether some number does, and not
whether the two ties happen to agree with each other.

**The nearby-signal search (2026-09-16).** `tools/puzzle/net806.py::nearby_signals` rewires net 806's
two consumers (`i0523.A1`, `i0525.A1`) to each candidate net in turn, on `verdict.Machine`'s own gate
list — the same evaluator every other stage uses, not a second simulator — and restores it after each
try. Candidates are nets driven by a combinational output pin within 15 µm of either consumer: **24** of
them. **5** reproduce `TWO NOT TOUCH` exactly on the first within-cap board and on all 23, and keep the
accepted board, `EMPTY SKY` and `BIG BANG` correct: nets **770, 785, 789, 951 and 984**. That is a real
test, not a search for something that works — the candidates are picked by geometry, not by trying
values until one matches, and the search could have come back empty.

**The review's earlier, unrestricted scan (historical).** Before this stage existed, review script
`r4_806_search.py` ran the same rewire against every non-supply net in the design — **738** of them, not
just the 24 within 15 µm — and found **23** that reproduce the message on the first board, the same kind
of survivors under the fuller check. That scan proves existence (something reproduces the message) but
is not, by itself, a test: trying everything cannot fail to find *something*. The geometric restriction
above is what turns it into one, and it still finds five of the strongest candidates, net 789 among them.

**Conclusion.** Net 806 is genuinely undriven **in the layout**: the extraction is right, the pin
assignment is right, and no missed merge hides a driver. But the layout does not determine what the net
carried, and nearby existing signals reproduce `TWO NOT TOUCH` byte-exactly — so "the design cannot
depend on it" and "byte-exact is unattainable" do not fit what was actually shown. Those words are
withdrawn; what replaces them is that the layout is silent on this one point, and at least five signals
already present on the die would have settled it.

This is also the *only* undriven net on the die (`netlist_check.json`: `undriven_nets: 1`), so the
layout is silent in exactly one place: one character of one of its five messages.

*The experiment as first imagined is kept below, superseded: the method actually used queried the
extraction engine's own net assignment rather than rendering neighbourhoods for the eye.*

Render the two readers' neighbourhoods from the layout — `tools/render.py` with
`recon/derived/pinmodel.json` — and test the pin assignment by hand: does the wire that is net 806 land
on the rectangle we call `A1`, and is the rectangle we call `Y` genuinely the cell's output? If `A1`
and `Y` are transposed for either master, the net acquires a driver and the message resolves; if they
are not, net 806 is genuinely floating in this layout and the published string cannot be produced by
our board class on this chip — a claim to state carefully, and only after that check.

## Reproduce this note

This note is now a **stage**, not a set of scratch scripts: the scripts could not survive a fresh clone
(`recon/scratch/` is gitignored, and the decisive experiment was not even listed here), which is the one
gap F6 was added to close.

```
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m tools.puzzle net806          # -> recon/derived/net806.json
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe tools/checks/check_stepF6.py    # re-derives every number below
```

`tools/puzzle/net806.py` (stage `net806`, plan step **F6**) rebuilds the extraction engine from A1/A2,
recovers the net by probing the two pin coordinates B4 recorded, files every shape on the die by the net
the engine assigns it, enumerates every cut over the wire **against the conductors its own A2 rule
joins**, measures the nearest `conb` output, re-asks the message tie through the E2 instrument, and
(`nearby_signals`) rewires the net to every combinational net driven within 15 µm of either consumer.
`check_stepF6.py` re-derives all of it — the geometry by a *second* method (a window census around the
pins instead of the whole-die sweep), the refutation and the distance live, both ties by forcing net 806
on a re-derived E2 board, the nearby-signal result, and one live rewire to net 789 that must reproduce
`TWO NOT TOUCH` exactly — and the fault grid's `net806:` mutations require that `stepF6` is the *only*
gate that fires when any of those claims is falsified.

The scratch probes (`recon/scratch/net806_probe[1-7].py`, `recon/scratch/n806_overlay.py` with its
output, and `r4_806_search.py`'s unrestricted 738-net scan) are kept as the record of how the
investigation was actually run. They are **superseded** by the stage, and nothing in this note needs
them to be reproduced.
